How “The Workshop” was made

Every illustration is hand-written SVG code, roughed up with a displacement filter until it looks like it was drawn with a pen — because warmth is a technique, not an asset pack.

StackHTML + CSS + ~15 lines JS
Illustrations100% inline SVG
FacesNunito + Caveat
Images usedNone

01.The wobble filter

The entire "hand-drawn" feel comes from one SVG filter, defined once and applied with a utility class. feTurbulence generates smooth noise; feDisplacementMap pushes every pixel of the drawing around by up to ~3px along that noise. Perfectly straight <rect>s and circles come out looking penned:

<filter id="wobble" x="-12%" y="-12%" width="124%" height="124%">
  <feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency=".035"
                numOctaves="2" seed="7"/>
  <feDisplacementMap in="SourceGraphic" scale="3.2"/>
</filter>

.wob{ filter:url(#wobble) }

That means the desk scene, the friendly machine, the magnifying glass, the bell/hard-hat/mug — all of them are ordinary geometric primitives (maybe 15 shapes each) that the filter makes charming. Drawing "badly" by hand is hard; drawing plainly and distorting is easy.

02.Sticker & sticky-note furniture

  • Cards — white, 2px ink border, and a hard offset shadow (box-shadow: 6px 6px 0) that reads as paper lifted off the page. Each card carries a half-degree random-looking rotation; on hover it straightens and lifts, which feels like picking it up.
  • Washi tape — a translucent mustard rectangle with a repeating-gradient stripe, absolutely positioned over the card's top edge.
  • Sticky notes — the before/after lists are two tinted note cards rotated in opposite directions, with ✗/✓ markers generated from CSS ::before.
  • Squiggle dividers — one quadratic-Bézier wave path (Q/T commands), stretched full-width with preserveAspectRatio="none".

03.Motion that feels analog

Nothing slides in from off-screen. Instead: the pain-list checkboxes check themselves as you scroll (the checkmark is a stroke with stroke-dasharray animated to zero offset when the row enters the viewport); the marker-highlight behind key phrases sweeps across using a scaling pseudo-element; coffee steam loops on a gentle opacity/translate keyframe; the plant bobs; the machine's antenna wags. All tiny, all loop-friendly, all disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.

04.The voice pairing

Nunito (rounded, sturdy) carries all real content; Caveat (a genuine handwriting face) is reserved for margin notes — "the pile…", "one clear view!", "that's both things we fix →". The handwriting layer works like a friend annotating the page over your shoulder, which is exactly the brand's operator-to-operator promise. Rule of thumb: if it's information, it's Nunito; if it's personality, it's Caveat.

05.Warmth without mush

The palette is terracotta, mustard, and sage on cream — but contrast discipline keeps it professional: all body text is a dark coffee ink (≥7:1 on cream), color is reserved for fills inside illustrations and accents, and the 2px ink outlines keep every element crisp. A fixed feTurbulence grain overlay at 5% opacity in multiply mode gives the whole page a paper tooth.

06.Lessons if you build one

  • One displacement filter turns programmer-SVG into illustration — spend your effort on what to draw, not how to draw it.
  • Hard offset shadows + thin ink borders read as "paper craft"; soft blurred shadows read as "software." Pick one world.
  • Rotate things 0.5–1.2°, never more. Past that, quirky becomes sloppy.
  • Handwriting fonts are seasoning, not soup. Two or three annotations per screen, maximum.
