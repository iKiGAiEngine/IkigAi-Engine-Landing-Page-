How the six cuts were built

Five new variations of the particle scroll-film, produced from one parameterized engine — the film-studio trick of reshooting the same script with a different director of photography.

One engine, six films

The original "From Noise to Signal" had its choreography hard-coded. For the variations, everything that makes the film feel different was pulled out into a config, and the engine (shared/cine.js, ~300 lines) became a reusable module. Each cut's page is just its themed markup plus this:

import { boot } from '../shared/cine.js';
boot({
  forms:    ['curtains','mountain','ribbon','helix','star','lightbulb','enso','logo'],
  palettes: [ /* one [r,g,b] ramp per formation */ ],
  spin:     .06,
  light:    false,
});

The engine works like the original film: every formation is precomputed on the CPU as a Float32Array and uploaded once as a vertex attribute (aP0…aP7); a single scroll-driven uniform morphs between them in the vertex shader with per-particle stagger. Each film runs eight formations mapped evenly across total scroll progress, so a morph is always in flight while the page moves — no dead stretches. Zero per-frame buffer updates — phones stay at 60fps.

The formation library

Formations come in two kinds now. Parametric clouds (nebula, vortex, waves, helix…) are a few lines of polar math. Recognizable silhouettes (clock, eye, bar chart, lightbulb, anvil, fish, star, mountain, envelope, document, checkmark…) are drawn on a hidden 2D canvas and pixel-sampled, so they read instantly, head-on. Every cut sequences seven of them plus the logo:

A few are worth stealing. The gear splits particles by probability into rim, teeth (quantized angle bins pushed outward), hub, and five spokes. The helix gives 12% of particles to "rungs" that lerp between the two strands, which is what makes it read as structure instead of two springs. The vortex is one line of polar math — radius shrinking as height falls while the angle winds 22 radians — but on screen it looks like a crucible pour.

The gradual logo finale

Each film ends on the actual brand — the brush-stroke enso, gold dot, "IkigAi" and "— engine —" — rebuilt in code (layered tapering arcs with seeded dry-brush streaks) so it stays crisp at any size and honest to the mark. The ending plays in three phases across a dedicated empty closing viewport: particles gather into the lockup's silhouette, sharpen as jitter and point size ease down, then resolve — the solid logo fades in at the exact projected position while every particle fades fully out. The last screenful is the clean logo alone. One geometry, two renderings: sand becomes signage, then the sand leaves.

The light-mode inversion

"The Paper Cut" is the technically interesting one. The other cuts glow because additive blending accumulates brightness against black — on a white page that math produces pure white, i.e. nothing. So the light cut flips to normal alpha blending with near-black ink particles at higher opacity, larger point sizes, and dark palettes. Same shader, two uniforms changed, completely different medium: it reads as ink on paper rather than light in the dark. The film grain overlay also switches from overlay to multiply blend so it darkens like paper tooth instead of glowing.

Art direction per cut

  • Palettes ride the same 5-slot color ramp system (one vec3 per formation, cross-faded during morphs) — night-sky silver→gold, smoke→ember→amber, murk→bioluminescence, auroral green→violet→pink, and ink→red.
  • Typography changes voice per cut: Fraunces/Inter (astronomy atlas), Space Grotesk/Plex Mono (industrial), Cormorant/Nunito (soft nature doc), Outfit/Inter (modern minimal), Fraunces/Space Mono (letterpress).
  • Act names re-skin the same story beats — the fog becomes the murk, the dark sky, the cold stock, the static — while every word of the conversion copy (pains, method, services, ownership pledge, CTA) stays identical. The metaphor changes; the pitch doesn't.
  • Motion personality comes from two scalars: idle-spin speed and the existing per-particle stagger. The forge churns a little faster; the current drifts slower.

The gallery hub

The six-cut gallery renders a live 2D "storyboard sketch" of each film's signature formation on a small canvas — 130 dots pushed through the same polar math as the real shader (vortex, waves, curtain, target, nebula), twinkling at ~zero cost. It's a preview of the choreography, not a screenshot of it.

Lessons if you build one

  • Parameterize after the first one works, not before. The original hard-coded film found the right architecture; the config fell out of it naturally.
  • Point-cloud "formations" are just probability distributions — learn to think in "X% of particles go to the rim, Y% to spokes" and any shape is 10 lines.
  • Additive blending is a dark-background technology. A light variant isn't a palette swap; it's a medium swap (blend mode, alpha, size, density).
  • When variations share one engine, every bug fixed is fixed six times — and every future cut costs one config and one afternoon of taste.
